Tactical guide

Video calls before meeting

A short video call has become a normal step between matching and a first date. Treat it as a quick, low-stakes checkpoint, not an audition, and it works in your favor.

Audience: adults 18+Read time: 5 minutesOutcome: a confirmed first date

Why this became normal

It's a safety check and a chemistry check, and both are reasonable.

Meeting a stranger from an app carries real safety considerations, especially for women, and a video call is a low-effort way to confirm someone is who their profile says they are. It's also just useful: text can't tell you much about how a conversation actually flows in real time. If she asks for one, it's not a hurdle. It's her doing normal due diligence, and passing it well is easy.

How to suggest a call yourself

You don't have to wait for her to ask. Suggesting one signals confidence and takes the pressure off the first in-person meeting. Frame it as practical, not as a test: "Want to hop on a quick call before we lock in plans, easier than texting logistics back and forth."

Running the call well

Setup

Decent lighting, a normal background, phone propped up

You're not producing content. You just need to be visible and audible.

Length

Ten to fifteen minutes, not a full call

The goal is a vibe check, not a second first date happening over video.

Content

Keep it light: how the week's going, logistics for the date

Save real depth for in person, same principle as texting.

Close

End with the date confirmed: day, time, place

A good call should produce a concrete plan, not just good vibes that fade.

What to avoid

Don't turn a quick check-in into a performance.

  • Over-preparing talking points like it's a job interview.
  • Letting the call run long past the point it's adding anything.
  • Reading a request for a video call as her being difficult or not trusting you; it's a normal step now.
  • Pressuring her to skip the call and meet in person immediately.
  • Ending the call without a concrete next step.

Next step
