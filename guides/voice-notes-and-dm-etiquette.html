Voice notes and DM etiquette

A voice note carries tone and personality that text can't. Used well, it's a shortcut to real connection. Used badly, it's an unwanted ten-minute monologue.

Audience: adults 18+Read time: 5 minutesOutcome: DMs that feel personal, not like a burden
Your voice note0:14 — "Okay that story was way too funny, tell me that's real"
Her reply100% real, I still can't believe it happened
1. Earn it firstVoice notes come after real texting, not before
2. Keep it under 30 secondsShort and reactive, not a monologue
3. Match the mediumReply to a voice note with one, not a wall of text
4. Respect the read receiptA seen message isn't an emergency

When a voice note actually helps

It works once there's already some rapport, not as an opener.

A voice note from a stranger, or someone you've exchanged three texts with, tends to feel like a demand for attention out of nowhere. Once you've built some real back-and-forth, a short voice note adds tone, humor, and personality that text genuinely can't. It's a good move to deepen a connection, a bad move to start one.

Length and content

Good length

Under 30 seconds, reacting to something specific

Long enough to convey tone, short enough that it's not a chore to listen to.

Too long

Multi-minute monologues about your day

Asks for more attention than the relationship has earned yet.

Good content

A reaction, a laugh, a quick story

Gives her something easy and enjoyable to respond to.

Skip this

Anything serious, emotional, or high-stakes

Save real conversations for a call or in person, where tone is less likely to be misread.

The unwritten rules of DM etiquette

SituationBetter moveWhy it works
She views a message but doesn't replyLeave it. Don't send a follow-up asking whyA view without a reply is still information
She replies to your storyRespond like a normal message, don't overanalyze the gestureStory replies are often low-stakes, casual interest
You want to send a voice noteAsk yourself if you'd be comfortable getting one back the same lengthReciprocity is a good gut check for what's appropriate
The conversation stallsLet it sit a day or two instead of sending reaction after reactionConversations have natural rhythms; forcing one rarely helps

Reading her signals

Effort and format are information. Pay attention to both.

If she's replying with voice notes of her own, matching that format is a good sign to keep going. If she's staying in short text replies even after you've sent a voice note, that's a signal to scale back, not a reason to send a longer one to compensate.

What kills a DM conversation

  • Sending a voice note before you've built any real texting rapport.
  • Long, rambling voice notes that require real time investment to listen to.
  • Treating a story reply as bigger news than it is.
  • Following up immediately after a message is marked as seen.
  • Escalating format (text to voice note to call) faster than she is.

Next step

Format is a tool, not a strategy.

Use whichever format actually fits the moment, and let the conversation's real momentum decide when to move toward a date.
